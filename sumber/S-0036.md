## Teks Asli
I drew the life of a developer curve again. It looks a lot like my own career.

𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝗳𝗶𝗿𝘀𝘁 𝗰𝗹𝗶𝗺𝗯

You start with math and Linux. Then C++, Python, Java. Then HTML, JavaScript, Git, TypeScript, React, Node, PostgreSQL, Next.js, Docker, AWS, and at the very top, Kubernetes.
Every one of those took months or years. Every one felt like the last big thing you would need to learn.
Somewhere near the peak you tell yourself: finally, I am an irreplaceable dev.

𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝗱𝗿𝗼𝗽

Then Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Cursor, Copilot, and Codex arrived. Someone with two years of experience could ship in an afternoon what used to take a full sprint.

Everything on the first hill still matters. It is just no longer rare. Typing out the code stopped being the hard part of the job, and a lot of senior developers felt the ground move under them.

𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝘀𝗲𝗰𝗼𝗻𝗱 𝗰𝗹𝗶𝗺𝗯
The way back up has its own ladder:
→ Prompt Engineering
→ Context Engineering
→ RAG
→ Agentic RAG
→ Agentic AI
→ Harness Engineering

The last rung is where I spend most of my time now. A harness is everything around the model: tools, permissions, memory, evals, guardrails, and what happens when an agent fails halfway through a task.

𝗪𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝗜 𝘁𝗮𝗸𝗲 𝗳𝗿𝗼𝗺 𝘁𝗵𝗶𝘀
The second climb goes faster for people who did the first one. If you have debugged a distributed system , you already know how to think about an agent that calls the wrong tool. 

If you have run Kubernetes in production, sandboxing and rollback are familiar ideas.

The dip was real. So is the path out of it. And the line at the top of the chart is honest: the climb continues.

Which skill from the first hill do you think will matter most for engineers building agents five years from now?
