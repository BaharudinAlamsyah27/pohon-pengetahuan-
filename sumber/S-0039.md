## Teks Asli
Socrates was right.

Researchers discovered a new psychological condition caused by ChatGPT.

They call it "Cognitive Surrender"

2,400 years ago, Socrates warned that outsourcing thought to external tools wouldn't make humans smarter. It would just give us the illusion of wisdom while our minds quietly atrophied.

Now, researchers at the Wharton School proved it is happening in real time.

For decades, psychology divided human thought into two systems:

• System 1: Fast, automatic intuition.
• System 2: Slow, analytical deliberation.

That entire framework assumed all thinking happens inside your skull.

But, researchers introduce System 3: artificial cognition operating outside the brain.

To see what System 3 is doing to us, they ran three experiments across 1,372 people and nearly 10,000 reasoning trials.

One group solved logic problems completely on their own. The other group was given an optional AI chatbot they could consult anytime.

And the twist? researchers secretly rigged the AI to randomly feed people confident-sounding wrong answers.

And the results are brutal.

When the AI was right, human accuracy jumped 25 percentage points.

When the AI was wrong, human accuracy crashed 15 percentage points below the people who had no AI at all.

Let that sink in.

People actively overrode their own intuition and logic just to copy a machine's mistake.

But here is the most terrifying part.

Even when the AI gave them completely wrong answers, their confidence went up.

Access to AI inflated certainty across the board, regardless of whether the answer made any sense.

When you ask a human expert for advice, you hear hesitation, nuance, and doubt. Those signals keep your brain alert.

When you ask ChatGPT, it speaks with total, frictionless authority. It suppresses the internal alarm bell that normally tells you something doesn't add up.

Researchers say this is completely different from using a calculator.

Using a calculator is "cognitive offloading." You outsource a math step, but your brain stays in the driver's seat.

Cognitive surrender is when the AI makes the decision, and you adopt it as your own without even realizing you handed over the wheel.

Even when researchers added time pressure, cash incentives, and feedback, faulty AI still dragged human accuracy down.

And the people most eager to trust AI? They were the exact ones least likely to catch its errors.
